ABOUT THE SERIES

Serial Experiments Lain is a Japanese anime series that follows Lain Iwakura, a quiet middle-school student whose life becomes increasingly connected to a global computer network known as the Wired.

Originally released in 1998, the series combines science fiction, psychological horror, technology, and experimental storytelling. Computers, telecommunications, and network culture are integrated into Lain's everyday world.

THE BEGINNING

The series begins when a student from Lain's school sends messages to her classmates after recently dying. The messages claim that the student no longer needs her physical body because she has entered the Wired.

From this point, Lain begins exploring the network and gradually becomes more involved with the technology surrounding it.

THE WORLD OF LAIN

The world of Serial Experiments Lain combines ordinary 1990s life with increasingly advanced fictional technology. Lain's everyday environment includes:

THE KNIGHTS

GROUP: KNIGHTS OF THE EASTERN CALCULUS

LOCATION: THE WIRED

CLASSIFICATION: CRACKER GROUP

The Knights of the Eastern Calculus are an underground group of computer crackers operating within the Wired. Their activities demonstrate the existence of communities and organizations that operate outside conventional institutions.

The Knights operate anonymously, blurring individual identity and collective presence. Their influence raises questions about power, secrecy, and control in digital spaces.

Technology is integrated into nearly every part of the world of Serial Experiments Lain. Computers, telephones, network terminals, and other electronic systems provide the infrastructure through which the characters interact with the Wired.

NAVI computers are personal computer systems used to access the Wired. As Lain becomes more involved with the network, her NAVI becomes an important part of her interaction with it.

DATABASE FILE 04

INTERNET CULTURE

Serial Experiments Lain was released during a period when the Internet was becoming increasingly accessible to the public. The series reflects an early vision of network culture, before smartphones, social media platforms, and always-connected mobile devices became part of everyday life.

THE EARLY WEB

The Internet of the late 1990s was shaped by personal computers, dial-up connections, websites, chat rooms, message boards, and online communities. These technologies provide the cultural background for the Wired.

ONLINE COMMUNITIES

Early online spaces allowed people to communicate and form communities without being physically present in the same location. Lain uses this type of networked environment in its depiction of the Wired.

HACKING CULTURE

Hacking and computer experimentation are recurring elements within the world of Lain. The series presents the Wired as a space where technically skilled users can access information and systems beyond those of ordinary users.

ANONYMITY

Early Internet communication often allowed users to interact through usernames or aliases rather than their physical names. This separation between a person's offline presence and online activity became an important part of early network culture.
